#Git #History #DevOps #OpenSource

>  [[Git]] was created in **2005** by **Linus Torvalds** (the creator of Linux) out of frustration. The proprietary version control system the Linux kernel team was using, BitKeeper, revoked their free license. Unhappy with the existing alternatives, Linus took a couple of weeks off to build his own, and the result was Git.

## 👨‍💻 The Creator: Linus Torvalds

The story of Git begins with a legendary engineer: **Linus Torvalds**.

Already world-famous as the creator and principal developer of the **Linux kernel**, Linus is a massive proponent of the free and open-source software movement.

## 🔥 The Catalyst: The BitKeeper Dispute

In the early 2000s, the developers of the Linux kernel were using a distributed [[VCS]] called **BitKeeper**. While it was a proprietary, commercial tool, its creators allowed the Linux team to use it for free.

> [!danger] The Breakup
> In 2005, a disagreement erupted between the Linux community and the company behind BitKeeper. The company decided to revoke the free license, effectively demanding that the Linux team start paying for the tool.

This put Linus in a difficult position. He needed a VCS that was:
1.  **Distributed** (like BitKeeper).
2.  **Fast** (a major requirement for a project the size of the Linux kernel).
3.  **Free and Open-Source** (a core philosophical principle for him).

At the time, no tool fit that bill. Existing free tools weren't powerful enough, and powerful tools were proprietary.

> [!success] The "I'll Do It Myself" Moment
> Unhappy with the available options, Linus Torvalds decided to solve the problem himself. He famously took a break from Linux kernel development, and in an incredible sprint during **April 2005**, he wrote the initial version of Git.

While he handed off the role of lead maintainer after a few months, the core architecture and design born from that sprint became the foundation for the tool that over 90% of developers use today.

## 📜 What's in a Name? The Meaning of "Git"

The name "Git" has a famously self-deprecating and humorous origin story, reflecting Linus's personality. According to the official source code and various interviews, the name can mean different things depending on your mood.

> [!info] The Official Meanings
> - **Practical:** A random, pronounceable three-letter combination that wasn't already a common Unix command.
> - **Self-Deprecating:** Linus initially called it "the stupid content tracker." In British English slang, a "git" is an unpleasant, stupid, or contemptible person. Linus has sarcastically quipped: *"I'm an egotistical bastard, and I name all my projects after myself. First 'Linux', now 'Git'."*

The official `README` file in Git's source code offers a more humorous take:
-   **When it works:** **G**lobal **I**nformation **T**racker.
-   **When it breaks:** **G**oddamn **I**diotic **T**ruckload of S\*\*\*.

> [!summary] Legacy
> The story of Git is a powerful testament to the impact of open-source software. A single, frustrated (but exceptionally talented) developer set out to solve his own problem, and in doing so, created a tool that would go on to revolutionize the entire software industry.
